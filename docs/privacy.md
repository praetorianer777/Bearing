# Privacy

What Bearing keeps about people, who reads it, and what it does not keep.
Bearing models an organisation, which needs some facts about the people in
it; it keeps those and no more, and says here who sees each.

## What everybody signed in reads

The organisation as a structure: units, positions, reporting lines, roles,
processes and their responsibilities, and who holds which position or role
from when until when, at what share of their week. This is what an
organisation chart shows, and what Bearing exists to answer. Readers,
members, editors and administrators read it alike.

A person's entry carries their name and, where known, an address, a phone
number, a location and a photo. Administrators decide which of these
fields people may change on their own entry.

Sums over a unit, a position or a role, such as capacity in full-time
equivalents, targets and gaps, are part of the structure and read the same
way.

## What is for fewer people

- **A person's capacity** over time, and what partners add to it later,
  such as their load from Armature and the days they are away: the person
  themselves, those above them in the management chain today, and editors.
  The database answers the question, so no client can read around it.
- **The change log**: editors and administrators.
- **Accounts, invitations, sign-in links and tokens**: each person their
  own; administrators manage accounts but never see a password or a token.

## What Bearing does not keep

- Why somebody is away. Absences, when Bearing reads them from Armature,
  are days only, never a reason, as in Armature.
- Performance, salary, contract terms or anything about the person beyond
  the facts above.
- A copy of another tool's data as the truth. What Bearing reads from a
  partner it may cache briefly, keyed by the realm's issuer and `sub`.

## Leavers

A person who leaves is marked gone from a day; their holdings end there and
the days before keep them, so the organisation can still be read as it
was. An administrator may unlink their account. Removing an entry with its
history is a database operation, so far not offered in the client.
